## About this code

OGEA-102 is The Open Group's number for the English **TOGAF Enterprise Architecture Part 2** exam (French OGEA-F102, Simplified Chinese OGEA-C102). Passing it, with Part 1 already passed, earns **TOGAF Enterprise Architecture Practitioner**, based on the TOGAF Standard, 10th Edition. There are three routes to the same credential, per the [certification portfolio page](https://www.opengroup.org/certifications/togaf-certification-portfolio):

- TOGAF EA Foundation holders take OGEA-102 (this exam).
- TOGAF 9 Certified holders take the Bridge exam OGEA-10B.
- Everyone else takes OGEA-101 then OGEA-102, or the combined OGEA-103.

### How the objectives above were built

The eight top-level objectives are the "major topic areas based on the Level 2 Syllabus" listed in the [TOGAF Portfolio FAQ](https://www.opengroup.org/certifications/togaf-portfolio-faq). The Open Group publishes no weight per area. The FAQ adds that some questions draw on several areas. The second-level items are the "Value of this Certification" statements from the official [Practitioner datasheet](https://certification.opengroup.org/docs/datasheets/datasheet_togaf_ea_practitioner.pdf), quoted as written. The datasheet lists them in its own order; placing each one under a topic area is this library's grouping, not The Open Group's. The detailed learning outcomes (8 units and 68 outcomes at Level 2, per the FAQ) are in [X2202](https://publications.opengroup.org/x2202), which needs a free Open Group account to download.

## Exam format

All facts are as of 2026-10-24, from the [TOGAF exams datasheet](https://certification.opengroup.org/docs/datasheets/togaf-exams.pdf), the [Portfolio FAQ](https://www.opengroup.org/certifications/togaf-portfolio-faq) and the [fee schedule](https://certification.opengroup.org/docs/exam-fee-schedule-retail.pdf). Confirm them before you book.

- **Question type:** scenario-based "complex multiple choice" with **gradient scoring**. Each question is a paragraph describing a real-world situation, followed by four answers. The best answer scores 5 points, the second best 3, the third best 1, and the distracter 0.
- **Number of questions:** 8 (40 points available).
- **Duration:** 90 minutes. For eligible English-as-a-second-language (ESL) candidates the time extension is allocated automatically after booking (FAQ).
- **Passing score:** 60%, which is 24 of 40 points.
- **Book policy:** **open book**. An electronic copy of the TOGAF Enterprise Architecture body of knowledge is built into the exam (the REFERENCE button). No paper books are allowed.
- **Price:** USD 405 booked directly at Pearson VUE, or USD 435 as a retail voucher from The Open Group shop. The combined OGEA-103 (Part 1 + Part 2, 48 questions, 150 minutes) costs USD 610 booked directly. Fees have applied since 1 February 2026.
- **Languages:** English, French and Simplified Chinese.
- **Delivery:** Pearson VUE test centres or OnVUE online proctoring ([online exams](https://www.opengroup.org/certifications/online-proctored-exams)).
- **Validity:** tied to the body of knowledge version, so it does not expire and needs no renewal (datasheet).
- **Applied badge:** if you take an accredited course and satisfactorily complete its mandatory *Learning Studies* (a minimum of 10 practical exercises), you also receive the **Applied TOGAF Enterprise Architecture Practitioner** Open Badge after passing (FAQ and Practitioner datasheet).

## What the exam really tests

Part 2 sits at Bloom levels 3 and 4, application and analysis. Each scenario describes an organisation (a merger, a regulatory change, a digital programme or a stalled architecture effort) and asks what the architect should do *next* or *how* to approach a phase. All four answers are usually reasonable. They differ in:

- **Fidelity to the ADM.** The best answer follows the ADM's intent for that phase: Phase A sets scope, stakeholders, vision and the Statement of Architecture Work, and gets approval before detailed design.
- **Stakeholder focus.** Strong answers identify stakeholders and their concerns and choose viewpoints for them. Weak answers jump straight to technology.
- **Governance and change.** Phases G and H answers turn on Architecture Contracts, compliance reviews and the change management process: is a change a simplification, an incremental change or a re-architecting change?
- **Requirements management.** Requirements are captured, stored and changed through the central Requirements Management process, not hard-coded in one phase.
- **Tailoring.** Iteration, levels (Strategic, Segment and Capability) and partitioning help you right-size the effort for the scenario.

**Where candidates lose points:** choosing the most *technically* impressive answer, skipping steps such as approval, stakeholder buy-in or the business case, and running out of time because they read the open-book reference instead of the scenario. Gradient scoring is forgiving (a second-best answer still earns 3), so steady judgement across all eight questions beats perfection on a few.

## Study strategy

1. **Start with Part 1.** Make sure the Part 1 vocabulary and phase objectives are solid before you start. Part 2 takes them for granted.
2. **Read the Practitioner material.** The Open Group's *TOGAF Enterprise Architecture Practitioner Study Guide* ([B255](https://shop.opengroup.org/study-materials/togaf/b255)) follows the Level 2 syllabus. Go deeper in the TOGAF Standard on Stakeholder Management, Phase A, Phases B–D, Phases E–G, Phase H, Requirements Management and the Architecture Content Framework. Look at the TOGAF Series Guides referenced in the Level 2 units, such as Business Scenarios, Value Streams and Business Capabilities, and Architecture Project Management.
3. **Practise scenario reasoning.** For each phase, write a one-page cheat sheet: purpose, inputs, steps, outputs, typical stakeholders, and "what goes wrong if you skip it". Then read case studies and rank four possible actions yourself.
4. **Consider accredited training.** It is not mandatory for the exam, but the Learning Studies are the only route to the Applied badge and give realistic practice ([training calendar](https://www.opengroup.org/training-calendar)).
5. **Know the reference before exam day.** The open-book material is an electronic copy of the body of knowledge, opened with the REFERENCE button. Learn how the body of knowledge is structured so you can jump to a phase's steps and outputs quickly instead of searching.

**How your background helps or misleads**

- *AZ-305-style design thinking*, which you will build during the Azure track, helps in Phases C and D but pulls you towards concrete solutions too early. TOGAF wants vision, stakeholders and the business case before the technology.
- *Agile and DevOps experience* (from AZ-204 and CKAD preparation) helps with the 10th Edition's emphasis on agile delivery and iteration. Do not assume that "agile" means skipping governance. TOGAF's answer is right-sized governance, not none.
- *Developer instincts* such as "prototype first" are often the second-best answer, not the best.

## Differences from related exams

- **OGEA-102 vs OGEA-101.** OGEA-101 has 40 closed-book recall questions at 1 point each. OGEA-102 has 8 open-book scenarios scored 5/3/1/0. OGEA-101 asks "what is X?". OGEA-102 asks "what should the architect do now?".
- **OGEA-102 vs CPSA-A (iSAQB Advanced).** Both are practitioner-level architect credentials, but the assessment style is very different. TOGAF Practitioner is a 90-minute exam. CPSA-A requires 70 credit points of module training, then a roughly 40-hour written design assignment on a realistic system and an oral defence with two examiners. CPSA-A proves you can design and document *one system* well. TOGAF Practitioner proves you can run an *enterprise architecture method*.
- **OGEA-102 vs AZ-305 and SAP-C02/SAP-C03.** All three use scenarios, but the cloud exams want the service and configuration that best meets stated constraints on one platform. TOGAF scenarios never mention products. They test process, stakeholders and governance choices. A strong cloud architect can still struggle with TOGAF Part 2 by reasoning about technology instead of method.
- **OGEA-102 vs AIGP.** Both reward "the most defensible governance action". AIGP applies it to AI law, risk and policy. TOGAF applies it to architecture change. Practising one helps the reasoning style of the other.

## Exam-day notes

- **Time budget:** 90 minutes for 8 scenarios is about 11 minutes each. Spend 3–4 minutes reading the scenario and question, 4–5 minutes ranking the four answers, and keep the rest for a final review.
- **Read the question last line first.** Find out what is being asked (the *next* step, the *best approach* or the *most appropriate* deliverable), then read the scenario looking for the facts that matter.
- **Rank, don't just pick.** Remove the clear distracter (often an action that breaks the ADM or ignores stakeholders), then compare the remaining three on how closely they follow TOGAF.
- **Use the reference sparingly.** Use it to confirm a phase's steps or outputs, not to read chapters during the exam.
- **Common traps:** answers that start detailed design before Phase A approval; answers that treat a change request as a new ADM cycle when Phase H would classify it first; answers that leave out the Architecture Board or compliance review in Phase G.
- **Combined exam:** on OGEA-103 you cannot return to the Part 1 section once you finish it, and there is no break before Part 2.
