## What the exam really tests

Security+ is CompTIA's broad baseline exam in security. It tests vocabulary and judgement across five domains: General Security Concepts (16%), Threats, Vulnerabilities, and Attacks (24%), Security Architecture (19%), Security Operations (27%) and Security Program Management and Oversight (14%). Most objectives begin with *Explain*, *Summarize*, *Compare and contrast* or *Describe*. About a third begin with *Given a scenario*. The scenario objectives are where PBQs come from: change management (1.2), indicators of malicious activity (2.5), securing the infrastructure (3.2), mitigating controls (4.1), vulnerability management (4.3), identity and access management (4.5), automation (4.6), investigation data (4.8) and security awareness (5.6).

The depth is wide and shallow. You need to know what a control *is*, which category and type it belongs to, and when to choose it. You do not need to configure one on a specific product. Typical scenarios ask you to:

- classify a control (a security guard is physical and preventive or deterrent; a log review is detective)
- pick the best mitigation for an attack described in a few lines
- read a log snippet or an email header and name the attack
- order incident-response steps, or pick the next step
- choose between risk treatments (transfer, accept, avoid, mitigate), or between recovery-site types (hot, warm, cold)
- match a data role (owner, custodian, steward, controller, processor) to a responsibility

**What is new in V8.** Comparing the V8 objectives with V7 shows several changes:

- **AI gets its own objective.** Objective 2.6 lists AI threats: model manipulation, poisoning, prompt injection, jailbreaking, hallucinations, evasion, and session hijacking and code execution through AI tooling. Large language models (LLMs) are listed as an attack surface in 2.4. Objective 4.6 adds agentic AI, chatbots and AI-augmented baselines as automation capabilities.
- **New identity items.** The outline now names passkeys, Group Managed Service Accounts (gMSAs), emergency-access accounts and backup codes, and lists just-in-time as an access control model in its own right.
- **New cloud and repository items.** Cloud security posture management (CSPM) and IP address management (IPAM) appear as vulnerability identification methods, and secrets scanning appears under repositories.
- **New lures and attacks.** Quishing (QR-code phishing), deepfake impersonation, Rich Communication Services (RCS) and collaboration tools are now listed as threat vectors.
- **New incident-response and investigation items.** Negotiation appears as an incident-response activity (think ransomware), and log-parsing techniques appear under investigations.
- **The weights moved.** V7's domain weights were 12/22/18/28/20. V8's are 16/24/19/27/14, so governance and program management shrank and concepts and threats grew.

**Where candidates lose points:**

- **Near-synonyms.** Deterrent vs preventive, compensating vs corrective, RPO vs RTO, MTTR vs MTBF, data custodian vs data steward, SLA vs SLO vs MOU vs MOA. CompTIA writes distractors that are almost right.
- **"Best" or "first" questions.** Several answers are true. You must choose the one that fits the stated constraint, such as cost, speed, least disruption or the next step in the process.
- **PBQs.** These are often placed at the start of the exam and take far longer than their share of the score.
- **Protocol and port trivia.** This is less central than it once was, but secure-versus-insecure protocol pairs (for example LDAP and LDAPS, or SNMPv2c and SNMPv3) still appear among the answer options.

## Study strategy

**Order of study (foundations first):**

1. **Domain 1, the concepts.** Learn CIA, AAA, Zero Trust, the control categories and types, change management and PKI first. Every other domain uses this vocabulary.
2. **Domain 3, architecture and data protection.** Data states, classification, data roles, resilience and recovery metrics. Your AZ-900 knowledge of regions, availability zones and the shared responsibility model gives you a head start here.
3. **Domain 2, threats.** Threat actors and their motivations, threat vectors, vulnerability types, attack indicators and the new AI threats.
4. **Domain 4, operations.** This is the largest domain. It covers hardening, IAM, vulnerability management, monitoring, automation, incident response and investigations.
5. **Domain 5, governance.** Policies and standards, risk management, third-party risk, compliance, audits and awareness training. Study it last, but do not skip it; at 14% it is still worth about 12 questions.

**Hands-on work that pays off.** None of these labs needs to be elaborate:

- Generate a certificate signing request (CSR) and a self-signed certificate with OpenSSL, then compare file hashes with `sha256sum`.
- Read an SPF record and a DMARC record with `nslookup`/`dig`, and look up your own domain's DKIM selector.
- Run Nmap against a lab VM and read the output.
- Look at Windows Event Viewer security events and at a Linux `auth.log`.
- Set up MFA and a passkey on a test account, and walk through Conditional Access in Microsoft Entra ID.
- Write a five-line PowerShell or Bash script that automates user provisioning. This gives you something concrete to picture for objective 4.6.

**Official learning resources.** The [V8 page](https://www.comptia.org/en-us/certifications/security/v8/) lists CompTIA's own CertMaster products: CertMaster Perform, CertMaster Learn, CertMaster Practice and CertMaster Labs. The free [exam objectives PDF](https://lecbyo.files.cmp.optimizely.com/download/77f3bd3223ac11f180820e495f189928) is the most important resource of all. Print it and tick off each bullet. Its acronym list is part of the exam too.

**How your background helps or misleads:**

- **AZ-900** gives you the shared responsibility model, defence in depth and Zero Trust vocabulary. Security+ uses vendor-neutral words, though, so "Conditional Access" becomes *policy-driven access control*, "Defender for Cloud" becomes *CSPM*, and "Sentinel" becomes *SIEM*. Learn to translate in both directions.
- **AZ-204 prep** covered managed identities, Key Vault, shared access signatures and OAuth 2.0 and OpenID Connect flows. That maps well to 4.5 (federation, SSO, OAuth) and to secrets management. Be careful with one point: in this outline OAuth sits under *SSO*, next to SAML and LDAP, so do not overthink the distinction between authorization and authentication.
- **CKAD prep** helps with containers, secrets, least-privilege service accounts and microservices (3.1). Security+ treats these at a definitional level, so do not go deeper than the question asks.
- **.NET and Angular development** makes the application attacks (injection, XSS, CSRF/forgery, replay) and the secure-coding items (input validation, secure cookies, static code analysis, code signing) easy. The trap is that developers jump to code-level fixes when the question wants a control-level answer, such as a WAF, a policy or training.

## Differences from related exams

- **SY0-801 vs SY0-701 (Security+ V7).** The same five-domain shape and the same format (90 questions, 90 minutes, 750 to pass), with the reweighting and AI, identity and cloud additions described above. If you have started V7 material, most of it carries over. Add objective 2.6 and the new bullets.
- **SY0-801 vs CS0-004 (CySA+).** CySA+ is the next CompTIA step on the defensive side. It drops governance breadth and goes deep on SOC work: reading tool output from Nmap, Nessus, Burp Suite, Prowler and Trivy, scoring with CVSS and EPSS, and running the whole incident-response process. Its exam is 165 minutes.
- **SY0-801 vs CY0-001 (SecAI+).** Security+ V8 gives AI one objective and treats it as a threat category. SecAI+ gives 40% of its exam to securing AI systems: guardrails, prompt firewalls, MITRE ATLAS, OWASP LLM Top 10 and model access control. Take Security+ first. SecAI+ assumes it.
- **SY0-801 vs CAS-005 (SecurityX).** SecurityX is the expert-level, architect-and-engineer counterpart. It covers post-quantum cryptography, Terraform and Ansible security, OT and SCADA, threat modeling with ATT&CK and CAPEC, and pass/fail scoring with no scaled score.
- **SY0-801 vs SC-900 and SC-500 (Microsoft).** SC-900 is a fundamentals-level tour of Microsoft's security products. Security+ is vendor-neutral and deeper on concepts. SC-500 tests implementing those concepts in Azure, Microsoft Entra ID and Defender. Security+ teaches you *why*, and the Microsoft exams teach you *where to click*.
- **SY0-801 vs CISSP.** CISSP covers similar ground with a manager's lens and needs five years of experience. Security+ is the practitioner's baseline. Many people take Security+ first and CISSP years later.
- **SY0-801 vs KCSA (Kubernetes and Cloud Native Security Associate).** KCSA is a narrow, Kubernetes-specific security exam. Security+ mentions containers and microservices only as architecture models.

## Exam-day notes

- **Budget about one minute per question.** With 90 minutes for up to 90 questions there is no slack. If the PBQs appear first, flag them and skip ahead. Answer all the multiple-choice questions, then return to the PBQs with the time that is left. A half-finished PBQ is better than an empty one, because PBQs may award partial credit.
- **Read the last sentence first** ("Which of the following BEST…", "…FIRST?"). Then read the scenario looking for the constraint: cost, least effort, minimal downtime, or legal or regulatory.
- **Prefer the vendor-neutral answer.** If an option names a product feature and another names the control category, the category is usually the intended answer.
- **Do not change answers without a reason.** The first answer is usually right on definition questions. Change it only if a later question reminds you of a fact that proves it wrong.
- **There is no penalty for guessing.** Answer every question.
