## About this code

**PECB-42001-LI is this library's own code.** PECB (a certification body that runs ISO-standard credentials such as ISO/IEC 27001 Lead Implementer) gives this exam no short number. Its name is "PECB Certified ISO/IEC 42001 Lead Implementer". The code joins the vendor (PECB), the standard (42001) and the role (LI, Lead Implementer). It is not the Lead Auditor credential, which is a separate PECB exam and course.

**ISO/IEC 42001:2023** is the international standard for an **AI management system (AIMS)**. It is the AI counterpart of ISO/IEC 27001 for information security. It uses the same harmonised clause structure (4 Context, 5 Leadership, 6 Planning, 7 Support, 8 Operation, 9 Performance evaluation, 10 Improvement) and adds AI-specific controls in Annex A, implementation guidance in Annex B, AI objectives and risk sources in Annex C, and domain and sector use in Annex D.

### How the objectives above were built

The six domains, and every competency and knowledge statement under them, are transcribed from the **ISO/IEC 42001 Lead Implementer Candidate Handbook, version 1.5** ([PDF](https://pecb.com/pdf/candidate-handbooks/pecb-candidate-handbook-iso-iec-42001-lead-implementer.pdf)). PECB lists *competencies* (things you can do) and *knowledge statements* (things you know) side by side, not one under the other, so each domain has two groups here: x.1 Competencies and x.2 Knowledge statements. The weights are PECB's "% of the exam" per domain: 17, 5, 20, 18, 10 and 10 of the 80 questions.

## Exam format

Facts are as of 2026-10-24, from the [course page](https://pecb.com/en/education-and-certification-for-individuals/iso-iec-42001/iso-iec-42001-lead-implementer), the [candidate handbook](https://pecb.com/pdf/candidate-handbooks/pecb-candidate-handbook-iso-iec-42001-lead-implementer.pdf) and the [course brochure](https://pecb.com/pdf/brochures/4/iso-iec-42001-lead%20implementer-4p.pdf). Confirm them with PECB or your training partner before booking.

- **Question types:** multiple choice with **three options** (one key, two distractors), both stand-alone and scenario-based. A scenario has about five questions attached. 60% of questions (48) test comprehension, application and analysis, and 40% (32) test evaluation.
- **Number of questions:** 80.
- **Duration:** 3 hours (course brochure). Candidates taking a Lead exam in a non-native language can ask for 30 extra minutes on the day (handbook).
- **Passing score:** 70%.
- **Book policy:** **open book**. You may use a hard copy of ISO/IEC 42001, the training course materials and your personal notes from the course.
- **Price:** taking the exam without training costs USD 1,000, plus a USD 500 certification application fee for the Lead Implementer credential. Training (five days including the exam, worth 31 CPD credits) is priced by PECB partners and **includes** the first attempt, one free retake within 12 months, the application fee and the first year's annual maintenance fee.
- **Languages:** PECB publishes exam languages in its List of PECB Exams rather than in the handbook. The course sessions listed on the course page are in English.
- **Delivery:** online through the PECB Exams application with a remote invigilator, or on paper at a PECB partner's training venue. Be present at least 30 minutes before the start with a valid ID. Online multiple-choice results are instant; paper results take two to four weeks.
- **Credential levels (after passing):** Provisional Implementer (no experience required), Implementer (2 years, 1 in AI management, 200 project hours), **Lead Implementer (5 years, 2 in AI management, 300 project hours)**, and Senior Lead Implementer (10 years, 7 in AI management, 1,000 project hours). Every level also requires signing the PECB Code of Ethics. Apply within 12 months of the course.
- **Retakes:** no limit on the number of retakes, but PECB's Exam Rules and Policies set waiting times between attempts. If you fail, the results email lists the domains where you performed poorly.
- **Renewal:** certifications are valid for **three years**. You need the required continuing professional development (CPD) hours and the annual maintenance fee (AMF). Missing either leads to a 12-month suspension, then revocation.

## What the exam really tests

The exam tests whether you can lead an **ISO/IEC 42001 implementation project** from start to certification:

- **Context and scope:** internal and external issues, interested parties, the AIMS scope, and the organisation's role or roles with respect to AI systems (for example AI provider, producer or customer, using the ISO/IEC 22989 role names).
- **Planning:** AIMS policy and objectives, AI risk assessment and treatment, **AI system impact assessment**, gap analysis, project plan, and the **Statement of Applicability** (which Annex A controls apply and why).
- **Implementation:** designing controls, defining processes, documented information and records, communication, training and awareness, operations and change management.
- **Checking:** monitoring and metrics, internal audit programme, management review, and nonconformities (major vs. minor).
- **Improving and certifying:** root-cause analysis, corrective actions, continual improvement, choosing a certification body, and Stage 1 vs. Stage 2 audits and follow-up.

Domain 1 also checks AI literacy: subfields of AI, EU AI Act risk classes and ISO/IEC 22989 terms. Planning (25%) and Implementation (22.5%) carry almost half of the marks.

**Where candidates lose points:** confusing the AIMS *risk assessment* with the *AI system impact assessment*; mixing up which clause requires what (for example, management review is clause 9, corrective action is clause 10); treating Annex A controls as mandatory instead of justified through the Statement of Applicability; and spending too long looking things up because the exam is open book.

## Study strategy

1. **Buy and read ISO/IEC 42001:2023.** It is a short standard. You may bring a hard copy into the exam, so tab it by clause and Annex A control area. Read ISO/IEC 22989 terminology summaries for Domain 1. ISO/IEC 42005 (impact assessment) and ISO/IEC 23894 (AI risk management) are useful companions.
2. **Learn the management-system pattern once.** If you study ISO/IEC 27001 (for the security track), the clause 4–10 skeleton is the same. Then learn what 42001 *adds*: AI impact assessment, AI-specific objectives, data for AI systems, AI system life cycle and third-party relationships.
3. **Take the official course** (a PECB partner, classroom or eLearning). It is the main official preparation, and the course materials are allowed in the open-book exam. The [PECB course page](https://pecb.com/en/education-and-certification-for-individuals/iso-iec-42001/iso-iec-42001-lead-implementer) lists the five-day agenda: introduction and initiation, planning, implementation, monitoring and improvement with audit preparation, and the exam.
4. **Draft the key artefacts for one real AI use case:** scope statement, AI policy, risk assessment, impact assessment, Statement of Applicability, internal audit plan and a nonconformity report. The scenario questions follow this sequence.
5. **Use the sample questions** in the handbook to learn the three-option style.

**How your background helps or misleads**

- *AZ-900 and the Azure and AWS AI tracks* help you recognise AI technologies in the "AI landscape" analysis (Domain 4). But controls here are organisational processes, not Azure Policy or AWS Config rules.
- *Developer experience* helps with life-cycle controls, but the exam speaks the language of management systems: "documented information", "interested parties", "nonconformity". Learn those terms exactly.
- *AIGP study* (if you take it first) covers the "why" of AI governance across many laws. This exam covers "how to run a certifiable management system" for one standard.

## Differences from related exams

- **PECB-42001-LI vs AIGP.** AIGP is a 100-question, closed-book, broad exam on laws, frameworks (NIST AI RMF, OECD, EU AI Act) and life-cycle governance. PECB is an 80-question, **open-book** exam with three options per question, focused on implementing ISO/IEC 42001 as a certifiable management system. AIGP has no experience requirement. The PECB *Lead* Implementer credential needs 5 years' experience and 300 project hours, although you can hold the Provisional Implementer credential from day one. Renewal also differs: AIGP every 2 years with 20 CPEs, PECB every 3 years with CPD and an annual fee.
- **Lead Implementer vs PECB ISO/IEC 42001 Lead Auditor.** The Implementer *builds* the AIMS. The Auditor *assesses* it against the standard using ISO 19011 and ISO/IEC 17021-style audit practice. Domain 6 here only prepares the organisation for the audit.
- **PECB-42001-LI vs ISACA AAIA, AAIR and AAISM.** The ISACA AI credentials are add-ons for existing auditors (CISA), risk professionals and security managers (CISM). PECB is standard-centric and project-oriented.
- **PECB-42001-LI vs SC-100 and SC-500 or AZ-500.** Microsoft security exams implement technical controls in a product. ISO/IEC 42001 requires that controls exist, are justified and are evidenced. A Microsoft Purview or Defender for Cloud setting can be *evidence* for an Annex A control, but it is never the control's definition.

## Exam-day notes

- **Pace:** 80 questions in 180 minutes is about 2 minutes 15 seconds each. Answer from understanding first, and use the standard only to confirm clause numbers or Annex A wording.
- **Prepare your references:** a tabbed standard and indexed course notes save minutes per lookup. Online, the course materials are accessed through the PECB Exams app.
- **Scenario sets:** read the scenario once, then answer its five questions together. The facts you need are usually in the scenario, not in the standard.
- **Three options means elimination works.** Remove the option that skips a clause step (for example, implementing controls before the risk assessment), then compare the other two.
- **Watch the words.** "Shall" (a requirement) vs. "should" (guidance); "risk assessment" vs. "impact assessment"; "correction" vs. "corrective action".
- **Online rules:** no second devices, camera on, remote invigilator. Ask for the 30-minute non-native-language extension on the day if you need it.
