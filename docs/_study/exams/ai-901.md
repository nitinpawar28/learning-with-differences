## Exam format

All facts below are as of 2026-10-24, taken from the [AI-901 exam page](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/exams/ai-901/), the [certification page](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/azure-ai-fundamentals/), the [study guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/resources/study-guides/ai-901) and Microsoft's [exam duration and experience](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/support/exam-duration-exam-experience) page. Confirm them on those pages before you book, because Microsoft changes them without much notice.

- **Exam and credential.** One exam, AI-901, earns Microsoft Certified: Azure AI Fundamentals. It took over the credential from AI-900, which retired on June 30, 2026. The English version of the current skills outline is dated April 15, 2026.
- **Question types.** Microsoft does not publish a fixed mix. Its duration page shows samples of the formats you may meet (active screen, build list, case studies, drag and drop, hot area, multiple choice and labs). Its duration table lists labs only for associate and expert exams, so do not expect a lab on a fundamentals exam. The [exam sandbox](https://aka.ms/examdemo) shows every format.
- **Number of questions.** Not published for this exam. Microsoft says most of its exams have 40 to 60 questions.
- **Duration.** Fundamentals exams give 45 minutes of exam time inside a 65-minute seat time. The AI-901 page itself does not print a duration, so check the time shown when you register.
- **Passing score.** 700 on a scale of 1 to 1000. This is a scaled score, not 70% correct.
- **Price.** Shown only after you pick the country where you will test. Pricing varies by country and excludes tax and promotions.
- **Languages.** English, Arabic (Saudi Arabia), Chinese (Simplified), Chinese (Traditional), French, German, Indonesian (Indonesia), Italian, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese (Brazil), Russian and Spanish. Localised versions are updated about eight weeks after English. If your language is missing you can ask for 30 extra minutes.
- **Delivery.** Pearson VUE, either at a test centre or online with a proctor (see Microsoft's [online exams](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/online-exams) page). Register with a personal Microsoft account, not a work or school account. Microsoft warns that records tied to an organisational account are lost if you leave the organisation.
- **Learn access.** Microsoft Learn is *not* available during fundamentals exams, unlike associate and expert exams.
- **Renewal.** Fundamentals certifications do not expire ([renewal page](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/renew-your-microsoft-certification)).
- **Retirement.** The exam page shows "Retirement date: none".
- **Stale summary.** The short description in the exam page's "Schedule exam" box still lists AI-900's old topics (machine learning principles, NLP workloads and so on). The study guide and the "Assessed on this exam" list are the current scope.
- **Practice.** A free practice assessment is available through AI Skills Navigator. You must be signed in to launch it.

## What the exam really tests

AI-901 is not a renamed AI-900. AI-900 was a "describe" exam. It asked you to recognise workloads (vision, NLP, conversational AI, classic machine learning) and match them to Azure AI services. AI-901 keeps that recognition layer in domain 1 (40–45%), but most of the score (55–60%) now comes from a **"build a lightweight app in Microsoft Foundry"** domain. The audience profile asks for Python syntax, and the notes say you should be familiar with REST APIs, SDKs and CLIs. For a fundamentals exam, that is a real change.

The themes:

- **Responsible AI as six named principles.** These are fairness, reliability and safety, privacy and security, inclusiveness, transparency and accountability. You must match a scenario to the principle it raises, for example a loan model that disadvantages a group (fairness), or a user who needs to know why a decision was made (transparency).
- **How generative models work, at concept level.** This covers tokens, prompts, the system and user message split, model choice by capability (chat, reasoning, multimodal, image generation, embeddings) and deployment configuration parameters such as temperature, max tokens and deployment type. You need to know which knob does what, not the maths.
- **Workload recognition.** You map a scenario to text analysis (keywords, entities, sentiment, summarisation), speech (recognition compared with synthesis), vision (understanding an image compared with generating one), information extraction (documents, forms, images, audio, video) or agentic AI.
- **Foundry hands-on flow.** You deploy a model in the Foundry portal, chat with it, then call it from a small Python client using the Foundry SDK. You also create a single agent in the portal and call it from a client. Expect questions that show a code fragment or a portal screen and ask which step, setting or value is missing.
- **Foundry Tools by name.** The tools named are Azure Speech in Foundry Tools and Azure Content Understanding in Foundry Tools. Many of the older service names are gone from the outline (Language Understanding, Bot Service, Form Recognizer and Anomaly Detector). The documentation list in the study guide still links some of those older pages, so do not take it as a sign they are in scope.

Where candidates lose points:

- They answer with the 2024-era answer (a dedicated Computer Vision or Form Recognizer resource) when the outline's answer is "a multimodal model deployed in Foundry" or "Content Understanding".
- They mix up speech *recognition* (speech to text) with speech *synthesis* (text to speech), and image *understanding* with image *generation*.
- They treat system prompts and user prompts as interchangeable. The exam expects you to know that the system prompt sets behaviour, persona and constraints.
- They skip the code entirely because "it's only fundamentals", then meet a client-code question.

## Study strategy

1. **Start with the concepts (domain 1).** Learn the six responsible AI principles until you can apply each to a short scenario. Then learn generative AI basics (tokens, context, prompts, temperature, grounding) and the workload families. This part builds on your AZ-900 vocabulary for resources, regions and pricing.
2. **Get a Foundry project working early.** Use a free or pay-as-you-go Azure subscription. Create a Foundry resource and project, deploy a chat model and a multimodal model, and use the portal playgrounds. Then write the smallest possible Python chat client with the Foundry SDK and keyless authentication (`DefaultAzureCredential`). That one exercise covers several bullets.
3. **Build one agent.** Create a single agent in the portal with instructions and a tool, test it, then call it from a client. Agents are central to the whole Microsoft AI track: AI-103, AI-500 and AI-300 all assume this basic loop.
4. **Do one small exercise per modality.** Do text analysis by prompting a model and by using Foundry Tools. With speech, respond to a spoken prompt using a multimodal model, and build a short Azure Speech app. Interpret an image in a prompt and generate an image. Then run Content Understanding on a PDF form, an image and a short audio or video clip.
5. **Use the official resources.** The self-paced learning paths are on the [AI-901 exam page](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/exams/ai-901/) ("Two ways to prepare"). There is the instructor-led course [AI-901T00: Introduction to AI in Azure](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/training/courses/ai-901t00), the free practice assessment in AI Skills Navigator, and the [exam sandbox](https://aka.ms/examdemo).

How your background helps or misleads:

- **AZ-900** gives you the Azure resource model, subscriptions, regions, pricing and the shared-responsibility mindset. It does not cover anything AI-specific.
- **AZ-204 prep** helps a lot with the SDK side: credential objects, environment variables for endpoints, async clients and REST calls. The trap is over-engineering. AI-901 wants "lightweight" clients, not production patterns.
- **.NET and Angular.** The exam assumes Python. Read the Python samples, even though the concepts carry over to the .NET SDKs. Your front-end experience helps you picture the "lightweight client application" items.
- **CKAD prep** is mostly irrelevant here.

## Differences from related exams

- **AI-901 vs AI-900 (predecessor).** AI-900 had five "describe" areas, including fundamental principles of machine learning on Azure (regression, classification, clustering, Azure Machine Learning). AI-901 drops classic machine learning as its own area, merges the workloads into one concepts domain, and adds a hands-on Foundry domain with SDK clients and agents. AI-900 was recognition-only. AI-901 is recognition plus light implementation.
- **AI-901 vs AIF-C01 (AWS Certified AI Practitioner).** Both are entry-level AI exams. AIF-C01 goes broader on theory and governance: machine learning fundamentals, the generative AI lifecycle, foundation model customisation choices (prompting, RAG, fine-tuning), responsible AI, and security, compliance and governance for AI on AWS. It names services such as Amazon Bedrock, Amazon SageMaker AI and Amazon Q, but never asks you to write code. AI-901 is narrower on theory, has no data-governance or compliance domain, and is more hands-on: you are expected to recognise Python client code and Foundry portal steps. If you take both, AIF-C01 rewards breadth of concepts and AI-901 rewards having actually clicked through Foundry.
- **AI-901 vs AZ-900.** AZ-900 is about the cloud platform (compute, networking, storage, identity, governance, cost). AI-901 assumes that platform and adds the AI layer.
- **AI-901 vs AI-103 (next step).** AI-103 covers the same Foundry surface at associate depth. It adds planning and securing, RAG, evaluation, multi-agent orchestration, image and video editing, and custom speech. Think of AI-901 as the guided tour and AI-103 as the job.
- **AI-901 vs SC-900 or GH-900.** Those are other fundamentals exams in the library. They are similar in level but share no content apart from responsible-AI themes in Microsoft's security story.

## Exam-day notes

- With about 45 minutes for 40 to 60 items, aim for under a minute per question. Mark the code-reading items for review rather than stalling on them.
- No Microsoft Learn access on this exam, so memorise the six responsible AI principles and the modality-to-tool mapping.
- Read "generate" against "analyse" and "recognise" against "synthesise" carefully. Many distractors differ only in direction.
- When an option names a retired or renamed service (Form Recognizer, LUIS, Cognitive Services), it is usually a distractor. When the choice is between a multimodal model and a single-purpose tool, check whether the scenario asks for a quick answer from a model or structured, repeatable extraction (Content Understanding).
- When an item asks you to judge several statements one by one, judge each on its own merits. One true statement does not make the next one true.
- If you take a break, you cannot return to the questions you saw before it. Review your marked items first.
