## Exam format

ISACA's code for this credential is AAIR, and the exam has no separate number. It launched after AAIA and AAISM, and ISACA now lists a fourth AI credential, Advanced in AI Governance (AAIG), as a beta. ISACA's [launch press release](https://www.isaca.org/about-us/newsroom/press-releases/2026/isaca-launches-advanced-in-ai-risk-aair-certification-to-equip-it-risk-professionals) is dated April 15, 2026. All facts in this section are as of 2026-10-24. They come from the [AAIR certification page](https://www.isaca.org/credentialing/aair), the [AAIR exam content outline](https://www.isaca.org/credentialing/aair/aair-exam-content-outline) and the [AAIR exam candidate guide](https://www.isaca.org/-/media/files/isacadp/project/isaca/certification/aair/aair-candidates-exam-guide.pdf).

| Item | AAIR |
|---|---|
| Question types | Multiple-choice only |
| Number of questions | 90 |
| Duration | 2.5 hours (150 minutes) |
| Scoring | Scaled from 200 to 800. 450 or higher passes, as for all ISACA exams. Only scored items count, and there is no penalty for wrong answers. |
| Languages | English, Spanish and Chinese |
| Price | US$459 for ISACA members, US$599 for non-members, plus a US$50 certification application fee after you pass |
| Delivery | PSI test centres worldwide or online with remote proctoring. Candidates in India, Mainland China and Hong Kong must use a test centre. |
| Eligibility window | Six months from registration |
| Prerequisite | One of a long list of qualifying designations. These include the ISACA certifications CISA, CISM, CRISC, CGEIT and CDPSE, and others such as CISSP, CGRC, CIA, CRMP, CRMA, CERP, CRCM, PMI-RMP and various CPA and chartered-accountant designations. The page and the launch press release also describe candidates as having proven experience in IT risk or advisory roles. |

**Renewal.** As of 2026-10-24, ISACA's [CPE policy](https://www.isaca.org/-/media/files/isacadp/project/isaca/certification/general/cpe_policy.pdf) sets out what an ISACA Advanced certification requires each year:

- keep your prerequisite designation current
- earn at least 10 CPE hours a year, and 30 over each three-year period, aligned to the AAIR content outline
- pay the annual maintenance fee

The outline is printed undated in the 2026 candidate guide. Confirm all the details on the official pages before you book.

## What the exam really tests

AAIR tests whether a risk professional can **bring AI into enterprise risk management**. There are three domains: AI Risk Governance and Framework Integration (37%), AI Life Cycle Risk Management (21%) and AI Risk Program Management (42%). Their subtopics (1A–1F, 2A–2D, 3A–3F) are the objectives above. ISACA also lists 23 "other skills tested":

- evaluate model and solution risk (design, suitability, algorithms, training and drift)
- integrate AI into the ERM framework, the governance programs, the risk register, the control taxonomies and change management
- build an AI risk framework, with roles, policies and tolerance levels
- run risk assessments and recommend treatments
- assess regulatory compliance
- evaluate use cases against risk appetite
- monitor emerging risk
- conduct or evaluate threat and vulnerability assessments
- advise on AI risk in contracts, data usage and intellectual property
- evaluate AI risk within supply-chain risk
- address trustworthiness, ethics, bias, privacy, safety and ESG
- use AI to support the risk program itself
- build AI into incident response, BIA, BCP and DRP
- assess human oversight at critical decision points

**The style.** These are ISACA risk questions, as in CRISC. Look for the answer that follows the risk process:

1. identify
2. assess against appetite and tolerance
3. choose a treatment with a named owner
4. monitor through KRIs
5. report to the right level

Technical options are usually wrong when the scenario has not yet established the risk or its owner.

**Where candidates lose points:**

- **Treating AI risk as only security risk.** Domain 1F (trustworthiness, ethics and ESG) and 1E (regulation and legal issues) carry real weight.
- **Mixing up appetite and tolerance,** or inherent and residual risk. These are CRISC basics, and they are tested here in AI scenarios.
- **Third-party models (3E).** Using a foundation model through an API is a supply-chain risk, with contractual, data-usage and IP aspects.
- **The life-cycle domain (21%).** It is small, but it is very specific: design and procurement documentation, training and validation, decommissioning, and data and asset management.

## Study strategy

**Order of study:**

1. **Risk fundamentals.** If you do not hold CRISC, learn ISACA's risk vocabulary and process first. CISSP's risk-management domain is a reasonable substitute, and CISSP is on the prerequisite list.
2. **AI fundamentals for 1A and Domain 2.** Model types, the life cycle, drift, explainability and AI attack types.
3. **Domain 1.** Frameworks and strategy, ownership and accountability, policies and training, regulation (the EU AI Act, the NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001 and ISO/IEC 23894 for AI risk) and ethics.
4. **Domain 3, the largest.** Scenario identification, treatment, controls, metrics and reporting, supply chain, and continuity.
5. **Domain 2.** Map each life-cycle stage to the risks and controls in Domain 3.

**Official learning resources.** The [AAIR page](https://www.isaca.org/credentialing/aair) lists ISACA's own preparation materials: an online review course, a Questions, Answers & Explanations database with a pool of over 200 questions, the AAIR Review Manual (digital and print), a virtual workshop and a free five-question practice quiz. ISACA's [AAIR blog post](https://www.isaca.org/resources/news-and-trends/isaca-now-blog/2026/who-aair-is-for-and-when-its-the-right-credential) (April 6, 2026) explains who the credential is aimed at: experienced practitioners in governance, audit, security or enterprise risk roles at staff, manager and director levels, not entry-level professionals.

**How your background helps or misleads:**

- **Your architecture and development experience (AZ-204 prep, CKAD prep, .NET and Angular)** makes the life-cycle domain and the technical risk scenarios intuitive.
- **AZ-900's shared responsibility model** maps well to third-party AI risk: who is responsible for the model, the data and the prompts.
- **The misleading part.** AAIR wants a risk register entry, an owner and a KRI, not an engineering fix.
- **Eligibility.** Of the prerequisites, the one that fits your roadmap best is CISSP. Plan it before AAIR.

## Differences from related exams

- **AAIR vs AAISM and AAIA.** All three share the same format: 90 multiple-choice questions in 150 minutes. AAIR takes the *risk* view (42% on risk program management) and has the broadest list of prerequisites. AAISM takes the *security-management* view and requires CISM or CISSP. AAIA takes the *audit* view and requires CISA or an equivalent.
- **AAIR vs AIGP (IAPP).** AIGP centres on AI governance, law and privacy, for policy and legal roles. AAIR covers governance too (Domain 1), but places it inside enterprise risk management, with registers, appetite, KRIs and continuity.
- **AAIR vs CY0-001 (SecAI+).** SecAI+ is hands-on and security-only. AAIR is risk-management and includes non-security risks (bias, ESG, legal, reputational).
- **AAIR vs CRISC.** CRISC is ISACA's general IT-risk certification and qualifies you for AAIR. AAIR applies the same risk method to AI.

## Exam-day notes

- **Pace.** About 1 minute 40 seconds per question.
- **Use the risk process as your checklist.** Has the risk been identified? Assessed against appetite? Given an owner? Treated? Monitored? The correct answer is usually the next missing step.
- **Answer every question.** There is no penalty for guessing.
- **Remote proctoring.** Read the candidate guide's rules for online proctoring in advance.
