## Exam format

All facts below are as of 2026-10-24, from the [certification page](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/azure-ai-apps-and-agents-developer-associate/) and the [AI-103 study guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/resources/study-guides/ai-103). Confirm before booking.

- **Exam and credential.** One exam, AI-103: Developing AI Apps and Agents on Azure, earns Microsoft Certified: Azure AI Apps and Agents Developer Associate (level shown as Intermediate). It replaced AI-102 (Azure AI Engineer Associate), which retired on June 30, 2026. The skills outline is dated April 16, 2026.
- **Duration.** 120 minutes. The exam is proctored and may contain interactive components.
- **Question types.** Microsoft does not publish the question types for any exam. Its [exam duration and experience](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/support/exam-duration-exam-experience) page shows samples of the formats you may meet: active screen, build list, case studies, drag and drop, hot area, multiple choice and labs, plus problem-and-solution sets. Try them in the [exam sandbox](https://aka.ms/examdemo). The certification page says the exam may have interactive components.
- **Number of questions.** Not published. Microsoft says most exams have 40 to 60 ([exam duration and experience](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/support/exam-duration-exam-experience)).
- **Passing score.** 700 (scaled).
- **Price.** Set per country and shown when you choose your testing location. Taxes and discounts are not included.
- **Languages.** English, Chinese (Simplified), Chinese (Traditional), French, German, Japanese, Korean, Italian, Portuguese (Brazil) and Spanish. You can request 30 extra minutes if yours is not offered.
- **Delivery.** Pearson VUE, at a test centre or online ([online exams](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/online-exams)). Microsoft Learn is available inside the exam in a split pane. The timer keeps running while you use it.
- **Retakes.** You can retake 24 hours after a first failure. Longer waits apply after that ([retake policy](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/support/retake-policy)).
- **Renewal.** Annual, through a free open-book online assessment on Microsoft Learn in the six months before expiry ([renewal](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/renew-your-microsoft-certification)).
- **Practice.** A free practice assessment is available in AI Skills Navigator.
- **Next step.** This certification is the only prerequisite listed for the [Multi-Agent AI Solutions Expert](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/multi-agent-ai-solutions-expert/) (exam AI-500).

## What the exam really tests

AI-103 is **the AI engineer exam rebuilt around Microsoft Foundry and agents**. AI-102 was organised by Azure AI service (Vision, Language, Speech, Document Intelligence, Search, OpenAI). AI-103 is organised by what you build: plan and secure the platform, build generative apps and agents, then use vision, text, speech and extraction as capabilities of those apps. More than half the score comes from planning, managing, securing and building generative and agentic solutions.

Themes and depth:

- **Choosing the right building block.** You choose among an LLM, a small language model, a multimodal model and a Foundry Tool; between vector, hybrid and semantic retrieval; and among memory, tool and knowledge services for an agent. Many questions are "given these constraints (latency, cost, data residency, accuracy), which option".
- **Platform operations.** This covers deployment types and options, quotas and rate limits (tokens per minute), scaling and cost, CI/CD for Foundry projects, and security through managed identity, private endpoints, keyless authentication and role assignments. This is where the exam overlaps with an administrator's job.
- **Responsible AI as engineering.** You configure content filters and guardrails, run evaluators and safety evaluations, keep audit traces and provenance, and govern agents with oversight modes, approval steps and tool-access limits. The question is how you enforce the principles in Foundry, not what the principles are.
- **Agents in depth.** Agent instructions and goals, tool schemas, function calling, retrieval tools, conversation memory and threads, multi-agent orchestration, semiautonomous flows with human approval, and monitoring with error analysis.
- **Operationalising.** Prompt and parameter tuning, reflection and self-critique loops, tracing and token analytics, and combining models with rules engines.
- **Modalities as features.** Image and video generation and editing (inpainting, masks), captions and alt text, visual question answering, Content Understanding pipelines (single-task and pro mode), video segment analysis, structured JSON extraction from text, translation, speech as an agent channel with custom speech, and multimodal safety such as indirect prompt injection through text hidden in images.
- **Retrieval pipelines.** Ingest and index mixed content, enrich it with skills and OCR, configure hybrid and semantic ranking, and expose the index as an agent tool.

Where candidates lose points:

- They study the AI-102 service catalogue (Custom Vision projects, LUIS, QnA Maker, standalone Document Intelligence models) instead of Foundry models, Foundry Tools and Content Understanding.
- They know RAG in theory but not the knobs: chunking, vectorisation at index time compared with query time, hybrid compared with pure vector, semantic ranker, and filters.
- They treat safety as one content-filter checkbox. The exam spreads it across prompt shields, groundedness detection, evaluations, approval flows and tool permissions.
- They miss operational limits. A 429 error points to quota or rate limits, not a bug. Token usage drives cost. Regional model availability shapes deployment choice.

## Study strategy

1. **Foundations.** If you skipped AI-901, study its concepts first: model types, prompts, responsible AI principles and the Foundry portal flow. Make sure you know the Foundry resource and project model and how identities and roles are assigned to it.
2. **One generative app, end to end.** In Python, connect to a Foundry project, call a chat model, add RAG over an Azure AI Search index (hybrid search with semantic ranking), then evaluate it with the built-in evaluators for groundedness, relevance and safety. Turn on tracing and read the spans.
3. **Agents.** Build an agent with function calling, a search or knowledge tool and memory. Add a human approval step for one risky tool, then a second agent and an orchestration pattern. Watch the traces to understand turns, tool calls and token use.
4. **Platform work.** Deploy with different deployment types, hit a rate limit on purpose, configure private networking and keyless access, and run a Foundry project deployment from a CI/CD pipeline (GitHub Actions or Azure Pipelines).
5. **Modalities.** Run Content Understanding analyzers on a PDF, an image and a short video, with structured and markdown output. Generate and edit an image with a mask, caption a set of images, transcribe and synthesise speech, and translate text with both Translator and an LLM.
6. **Official resources.** The learning paths are linked under "Prepare for the exam" on the [certification page](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/azure-ai-apps-and-agents-developer-associate/). There is also the [study guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/resources/study-guides/ai-103), the [exam sandbox](https://aka.ms/examdemo) and the AI Skills Navigator practice assessment.

How your background helps or misleads:

- **AZ-900** gives you resource groups, regions, RBAC and pricing vocabulary.
- **AZ-204 prep** gives you managed identity, Key Vault, SDK authentication, App Service and Functions hosting, and CI/CD habits. All of these apply to "Set up AI solutions in Foundry". It does not prepare you for model selection, evaluation or safety.
- **CKAD prep** helps only indirectly, if you host agents in containers.
- **.NET and Angular.** Microsoft names Python as the expected language, so read the Python SDK samples. Your front-end experience helps with the accessibility items (alt text, image descriptions) and with thinking about human-in-the-loop interfaces.

## Differences from related exams

- **AI-103 vs AI-102 (predecessor).** AI-102 had six areas. Generative AI and agentic solutions were two of the smaller ones, and knowledge mining and information extraction (Azure AI Search and Document Intelligence) had a domain of their own. AI-103 moves the centre to generative and agentic solutions (30–35%) and planning, management and responsible AI (25–30%), and shrinks each modality to 10–15%. Content Understanding replaces much of the old Document Intelligence and Video Indexer material. Image and video generation are new.
- **AI-103 vs AI-901.** The same Foundry surface at a different depth. AI-901 asks you to deploy a model and build a lightweight client. AI-103 asks you to choose, secure, scale, evaluate, govern and orchestrate.
- **AI-103 vs AI-200.** AI-200 builds the back-end services around an AI app: containers, Cosmos DB, PostgreSQL, Redis, messaging, telemetry. AI-103 builds the AI inside it. The two overlap only on vector retrieval and observability.
- **AI-103 vs AI-500.** AI-500 is the expert step. It assumes AI-103 skills and adds architecture (decomposing goals into agents and tools, Zero Trust, multi-tier memory), open protocols (MCP, A2A), frameworks (Microsoft Agent Framework, LangGraph), release engineering and cost governance. AI-103 has a single multi-agent bullet. AI-500 spends a third of the exam on it.
- **AI-103 vs AI-300.** AI-300 is the operations view: Azure Machine Learning lifecycle, GenAIOps infrastructure, prompt versioning in Git, evaluation pipelines and fine-tuning. AI-103 evaluates and monitors too, but from the app builder's side.
- **AI-103 vs AIP-C01 (AWS Certified Generative AI Developer – Professional).** Both target the developer who builds generative AI and agent solutions on a managed model platform: Amazon Bedrock (Knowledge Bases, Agents, Guardrails) on AWS, Microsoft Foundry here. The AWS exam is professional level. It goes deeper on integration architecture, data and compliance, cost and performance optimisation, and testing and troubleshooting across many AWS services. It does not cover vision, speech and document extraction as separate domains. AI-103 is associate level but broader in modality: 30–45% of it is vision, text, speech and information extraction. Mapping: Azure AI Search to Bedrock Knowledge Bases or OpenSearch, Foundry Agent Service to Bedrock Agents, Foundry guardrails and content filters to Bedrock Guardrails, Content Understanding to Bedrock Data Automation or Textract.

## Exam-day notes

- You have 120 minutes. Case studies come with a long scenario, so read the requirements and constraints tabs first, then the questions, then the background only as needed.
- Microsoft Learn is available, but Foundry naming changes quickly and you have no time to explore. Use it to confirm a parameter name or a limit, not to learn a concept.
- Watch for retired names (Azure OpenAI Studio, Cognitive Services, Form Recognizer, LUIS) used as distractors next to the current Foundry names.
- "Least effort" usually points to a built-in Foundry capability (built-in evaluators, Content Understanding prebuilt analyzers, built-in skills) over custom code. "Must keep data private" points to private networking and keyless identity.
- Breaks are not allowed inside a problem-and-solution set or a lab. Take any break before or after, and remember that after a break you cannot return to questions you have already seen.
