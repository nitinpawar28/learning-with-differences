## About this certification

The **FinOps Foundation** (a Linux Foundation project) defines FinOps as "an operational framework and cultural practice which maximizes the business value of technology, enables timely data-driven decision making, and creates financial accountability through collaboration between engineering, finance, and business teams". **FinOps Certified Practitioner** is its entry certification. The Foundation uses no exam number, so FOCP is this library's short code. Higher credentials include FinOps Certified Engineer, FinOps Certified FOCUS Analyst, FinOps Certified: AI Value, FinOps Certified: Technology Value and FinOps Certified Professional. Per the training FAQ, you need a current Practitioner or Engineer certification to enrol in the Professional, and active FOCUS Analyst, AI Value and Technology Value certifications to sit its exam.

### How the objectives above were built

The FinOps Foundation does **not** publish an exam blueprint with domains or weights for FOCP. What it does publish is:

1. the **course curriculum** for the official self-paced course on [learn.finops.org](https://learn.finops.org/path/finops-certified-practitioner-self-paced): FinOps Today; Principles and Personas; The Framework in Motion; Capabilities in Action; Scopes and Technology Categories; Adopting FinOps. These six modules are the top-level objectives above.
2. the **FinOps Framework** itself ([finops.org/framework](https://www.finops.org/framework/), CC BY 4.0; the Foundation's 3 April 2026 post "2026 Framework Updates: Executive Strategy Alignment, Refining Scopes" is the latest change), which the course was "developed in alignment with". The second- and third-level objectives are the Framework elements named on that page (principles, personas, phases, maturity, domains with their capabilities, scopes and technology categories), each placed under the course module whose title matches it. That placement is this library's, not the Foundation's.

Modules 1 and 6 have no Framework element list, so they have no sub-objectives. Expect the exam to follow the current Framework vocabulary. Capability names change when the Framework is updated (for example, "Executive Strategy Alignment" came with the 2026 update).

## Exam format

Facts are as of 2026-10-24, from the [exam-only page](https://learn.finops.org/finops-certified-practitioner-certification-exam), the [course page](https://learn.finops.org/path/finops-certified-practitioner-self-paced) and the [training FAQ](https://www.finops.org/training-certification/training-faqs/). Confirm them before purchase.

- **Question type:** multiple choice.
- **Number of questions:** 50.
- **Duration:** 1 hour.
- **Passing score:** 75%.
- **Attempts:** three attempts within 12 months of purchase.
- **Proctoring:** **not proctored**. You take the self-directed exam online at any time.
- **Price:** USD 325 for the exam only. USD 500 for the self-paced course plus exam, which includes 12 months of course access. Instructor-led options and bundles (for example with FOCUS Analyst) are also listed. Many companies buy training subscriptions that include it.
- **Languages:** English. The course and exam are also offered in Japanese. Extended exam time is available for non-native English speakers through the training support form (FAQ).
- **Validity and renewal:** valid for **24 months**. Renew with the FinOps Certified Practitioner recertification exam, which the FAQ says is offered at a discount on the full exam price.
- **Credential:** a digital certificate emailed immediately after you pass, with a unique verification code, plus a Credly badge.

## What the exam really tests

FOCP is a **vocabulary and operating-model** exam. It does not test cloud consoles. Questions check that you can:

- recognise the six **Principles** and apply them to a situation (who should own usage, why data must be timely, why central enablement doesn't mean central control);
- match **Personas** to responsibilities and motivations (Engineering wants performance and speed, Finance wants predictability and accurate accruals, Procurement wants rate and contract leverage, Leadership wants value);
- place activities in the **Inform → Optimize → Operate** phases and judge **Crawl/Walk/Run** maturity;
- map a problem to the right **Domain and Capability**: tagging and showback → Allocation; a sudden spend spike → Anomaly Management; commitment discounts → Rate Optimization; rightsizing → Usage Optimization; cost per customer → Unit Economics;
- apply FinOps beyond public cloud using **Scopes and Technology Categories** (SaaS, licences, data centre, AI), the Framework's newer emphasis;
- know the basics of cloud billing data and **FOCUS** (the FinOps Open Cost and Usage Specification), which the course introduces.

**Where candidates lose points:** confusing similar capabilities (Planning & Estimating vs. Forecasting vs. Budgeting, or Invoicing & Chargeback vs. Allocation); treating FinOps as only cost cutting instead of maximising value; and using provider-specific terms instead of the Framework's neutral terms.

## Study strategy

1. **Read the Framework pages** on finops.org: principles, personas, phases, maturity, then each domain and capability page. For each capability, write one sentence on what it is and one on which persona leads it.
2. **Take the official course** (self-paced or instructor-led) if you can. It is short, and the exam follows its module structure.
3. **Map it to the clouds you know.**
   - *Azure:* Cost Management + Billing (cost analysis, budgets, anomaly alerts, exports), tags and management groups for allocation, Azure Advisor cost recommendations, reservations and savings plans for rate optimisation.
   - *AWS:* Cost Explorer, AWS Budgets, Cost Anomaly Detection, Cost and Usage Reports and Data Exports (including the FOCUS format), cost allocation tags, Compute Optimizer, Savings Plans and Reserved Instances.
   Use these to *understand* the capabilities, then answer in FinOps terms.
4. **Learn FOCUS at a high level:** what it standardises (cost and usage columns across providers) and why it helps allocation and reporting.
5. **Revise vocabulary before the exam.** With 50 questions in an hour and 75% to pass, recall speed matters.

**How your background helps or misleads**

- *AZ-900* already covered pricing, TCO, reservations and Cost Management basics, which is a solid start for the Inform and Optimize phases.
- *AZ-204 and CKAD preparation* make you think about engineering-side optimisation (autoscale, rightsizing, containers). Remember that most FinOps questions are about *process and accountability*, not technical tuning.
- *Architect ambitions:* the "Architecting & Workload Placement" capability links directly to the Well-Architected cost pillars on Azure and AWS. That is where FOCP pays off for AZ-305 and SAA-C03 reasoning.

## Differences from related exams

- **FOCP vs AZ-900 and CLF-C02.** The cloud fundamentals exams test *provider* pricing and billing tools (what a reservation is, where to find Cost Explorer). FOCP tests the *operating model* that uses them: who is accountable, how costs are allocated, how value is measured. It is vendor-neutral and covers non-cloud technology spend too.
- **FOCP vs AZ-305 and SAA-C03/SAP-C02.** The architect exams test cost-aware *design decisions* (choosing tiers, serverless vs. provisioned, storage classes). FOCP tests the *organisational loop* around them. The two complement each other: architects use FinOps unit economics to justify designs.
- **FOCP vs the AI governance exams (AIGP, PECB ISO/IEC 42001).** These address AI risk and compliance. FOCP's AI technology category and the FinOps Certified: AI Value credential address AI *cost and value*, which matters for the agentic-AI track (token spend, GPU capacity commitments).
- **FOCP vs FinOps Certified Professional.** Practitioner is a 50-question, 1-hour, unproctored foundation exam with no prerequisites. Professional sits at the top of the path: you need a current Practitioner or Engineer certification to enrol, active FOCUS Analyst, AI Value and Technology Value certifications to sit the exam, and a Professional Contribution showing participation and leadership in the FinOps community (training FAQ).

## Exam-day notes

- **Pace:** 50 questions in 60 minutes is 72 seconds each. Answer quickly and flag only real uncertainties.
- **Unproctored does not mean open-ended.** The timer is strict and three attempts are all you get in 12 months. Take it when you can recall the capabilities without notes.
- **Think "which capability or persona?" first.** Most scenario questions become easy once you name the capability involved.
- **Prefer value over savings.** If one answer cuts cost and another aligns spend with business value and shared accountability, the Framework favours the second.
- **Use neutral terms.** "Commitment-based discounts" covers Azure reservations and savings plans as well as AWS Savings Plans and RIs, and "allocation" covers tagging, account structure and shared-cost splitting.
