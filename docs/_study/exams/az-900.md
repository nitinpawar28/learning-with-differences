## You already hold this one

You passed AZ-900, so this page is a reference, not a study plan. It is useful in three ways: it records what the current outline (skills measured as of July 20, 2026) expects, it shows what has moved since you sat it, and it maps the vocabulary that every later Azure exam assumes you know. The [change log on the study guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/resources/study-guides/az-900) lists only minor changes in the July 2026 revision: to *Describe Azure compute and networking services*, *Describe features and tools for managing and deploying Azure resources* and *Describe monitoring tools in Azure*. The audience profile and the three domain titles did not change.

## Exam format

All facts below are as of 2026-10-24. Confirm them on the official pages before booking anything.

- **Duration.** The [certification page](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/azure-fundamentals/) gives 45 minutes. Microsoft's [exam duration and experience page](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/support/exam-duration-exam-experience) lists 45 minutes of exam time and 65 minutes of seat time for fundamentals exams.
- **Questions.** Microsoft does not publish the count or the formats in advance. The duration page says most Microsoft exams have 40 to 60 questions. The exam sandbox (linked from the certification page) lets you try the question types and the exam interface.
- **Passing score.** 700 on a scale of 1 to 1000 ([exam scoring and score reports](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/exam-scoring-reports)). The score is scaled, so 700 does not mean 70 percent of the questions.
- **Price.** The certification page says only that the price depends on the country or region where the exam is proctored. It shows the price for your location when you start scheduling.
- **Languages.** English, Japanese, Chinese (Simplified), Korean, Spanish, German, French, Indonesian (Indonesia), Arabic (Saudi Arabia), Chinese (Traditional), Italian, Portuguese (Brazil) and Russian. If your language isn't offered, the study guide says you can request an extra 30 minutes.
- **Delivery.** Proctored, through Pearson VUE (at a test centre or online). Students and educators can schedule through Certiport instead. Unlike role-based exams, fundamentals exams do not give you access to Microsoft Learn during the exam.
- **Retakes.** You can retake the exam 24 hours after a first failure. Waits get longer after that (see the retake policy linked from the certification page).
- **Renewal.** The certification page shows no renewal frequency, unlike the associate pages such as Azure Administrator and Azure Network Engineer ("12 months"). Microsoft's [renewal page](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/renew-your-microsoft-certification) states that fundamentals certifications do not expire, so the Azure Fundamentals credential you hold needs no renewal.

## What the exam really tests

AZ-900 tests vocabulary and recognition, not configuration. Every bullet starts with *describe*, *define*, *compare*, *identify* or *explore*. The exam checks whether you can match a requirement to the right category or service, and whether you know where Azure draws its lines:

- **Cloud concepts (25–30%).** The shared responsibility model by service type, public, private and hybrid clouds, consumption-based and other pricing models, serverless, and the "benefits of" list (high availability, scalability, reliability, predictability, security, governance, manageability). Questions often hinge on one word, such as *scalability* versus *high availability*, or *consumption-based* versus *reserved* pricing.
- **Architecture and services (35–40%).** The resource hierarchy (management groups, then subscriptions, then resource groups, then resources), regions, region pairs, sovereign regions and availability zones. It then covers the main services in compute (VMs, scale sets, availability sets, Azure Virtual Desktop, containers, functions, web apps), networking (VNets, subnets, peering, Azure DNS, VPN Gateway, ExpressRoute, public and private endpoints), storage (services, tiers, redundancy, AzCopy, Storage Explorer, File Sync, Azure Migrate, Data Box) and identity and security (Microsoft Entra ID, Entra Domain Services, SSO, MFA, passwordless, external identities, Conditional Access, RBAC, Zero Trust, defence in depth, Defender for Cloud).
- **Management and governance (30–35%).** Cost factors, the pricing calculator, Cost Management and tags. Then Microsoft Purview, Azure Policy and resource locks. Then the portal, Cloud Shell, CLI, PowerShell, Azure Arc, infrastructure as code, ARM and ARM templates. Finally Advisor, Service Health and Azure Monitor (Log Analytics, alerts, Application Insights).

Candidates lose points on near-synonyms (Azure Policy versus RBAC versus resource locks, Service Health versus Advisor), on storage redundancy letters (LRS, ZRS, GRS, GZRS and the read-access variants), and on which scope a setting is inherited from.

## Study strategy

You are past this exam, so use it as a foundation check before AZ-104. Go back over the official [AZ-900T00 course](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/training/courses/az-900t00) and its self-paced learning paths (for example [Describe cloud concepts](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/training/paths/microsoft-azure-fundamentals-describe-cloud-concepts/)). Take the free practice assessment linked from the certification page to see whether the July 2026 wording still feels familiar. For each AZ-900 service you can *describe*, ask whether you could *configure* it. That gap is exactly what AZ-104 covers.

How your background helps or misleads:

- **AZ-204 preparation** gave you App Service, Functions, containers, Cosmos DB and Key Vault at a depth well beyond AZ-900. Don't over-read the fundamentals questions. They want the category, not the SDK detail.
- **CKAD preparation** makes "containers" feel like Kubernetes. AZ-900 compares containers with VMs and functions at a conceptual level. Kubernetes (AKS) is not a named bullet.
- **Basic AWS knowledge** maps most concepts directly (Region and availability zone, IAM and RBAC, CloudWatch and Azure Monitor). Watch the differences: Azure has management groups and resource groups as first-class scopes, region pairs, and identity in a Microsoft Entra tenant rather than inside each account.

## Differences from related exams

- **AZ-900 vs CLF-C02 (AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner).** Both are entry exams about recognition and vocabulary. CLF-C02 puts more weight on billing, pricing and support plans, and on the AWS Well-Architected pillars. AZ-900 spends more of its weight on governance tools (Policy, locks, Purview, Arc) and on Microsoft Entra identity.
- **AZ-900 vs AZ-104.** The same services, one level deeper. *Describe storage redundancy* becomes *configure Azure Storage redundancy, object replication and lifecycle management*, and *describe RBAC* becomes *assign roles at different scopes and interpret access assignments*. AZ-104 also adds Bicep and ARM template editing, backup and Site Recovery, and network troubleshooting.
- **AZ-900 vs AI-901 and SC-900.** These are the sibling fundamentals exams for AI workloads and for security, compliance and identity. SC-900 takes the identity part of AZ-900 (Entra ID, Conditional Access, Zero Trust) much further, into Microsoft Purview and Defender.

## Exam-day notes

- Forty-five minutes is enough for most candidates, but the questions are short, so it is easy to rush. Read every word of the requirement. "Minimise cost" and "minimise administrative effort" point to different answers.
- Watch for absolute words such as *always* and *only*, and for answers that name a real Azure service that does a different job.
- There is no Microsoft Learn access in fundamentals exams, so you can't look anything up.
