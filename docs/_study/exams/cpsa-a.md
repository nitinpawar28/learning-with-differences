## About this certification

**CPSA-A** is iSAQB's Advanced Level. It is not one exam with one syllabus. It is a **modular programme** followed by a **practical examination**. iSAQB has no exam number for it, so the library uses the scheme's own abbreviation. You collect credit points by attending licensed module trainings. Each module awards points in three competence areas: **METHOD** (methodical), **TECH** (technological) and **COMM** (communicative). With at least 70 points and the other admission criteria, you apply to a certification body, receive a design task, write a solution, and defend it to two examiners.

### How the objectives above were built

- **Objective 1** is the programme structure and exam, from the [Advanced Level exam page](https://www.isaqb.org/certifications/cpsa-exams/advanced-level-exam/) and the [Advanced Level Examination Rules](https://www.isaqb.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/iSAQB_CPSA_Advanced_Level_Examination_Rules_EN_02-09-2026-1.pdf) (dated 02-09-2026).
- **Objectives 2 and 3** are the two modules most relevant to this library's agentic-AI track. Each chapter is a second-level objective and each learning goal (LG) a third-level objective, numbered as in the curriculum:
  - **SWARC4AI – Software Architecture for AI Systems** ([module page](https://www.isaqb.org/certifications/cpsa-certifications/cpsa-advanced-level/swarc4ai/), [curriculum PDF](https://public.isaqb.org/curriculum-swarc4ai/curriculum-swarc4ai-en.pdf), version 2024.1-rev1-EN-20250208), worth 20 TECH + 10 METHOD points. LGs marked [OPTIONAL] are optional training content in the curriculum. LG 5-1 and LG 5-5 have the same title in the curriculum, and both are kept.
  - **AGENTA – Architecture for Agentic Software Engineering Contexts** ([module page](https://www.isaqb.org/certifications/cpsa-certifications/cpsa-advanced-level/agenta-architecture-for-agentic-engineering-contexts/), [curriculum PDF](https://public.isaqb.org/curriculum-agenta/curriculum-agenta-en.pdf), version 2026.1-rev5-EN-20260731), worth 10 TECH + 20 METHOD points.

The other 20 modules each have their own curriculum on the [Advanced Level page](https://www.isaqb.org/certifications/cpsa-certifications/cpsa-advanced-level/). As of 2026-10-24 the list includes ADOC, AGILA, API, ARCEVAL, CLOUDINFRA, COSMO, DDD, DSL, EAM, EMBEDDED, EMBEDDEDSEC, FLEX, FM, FUNAR, GREEN, IMPROVE, REQ4ARC, SOFT, WEB and WEBSEC. SWARC4AI + AGENTA give 60 points (TECH 30, METHOD 30) and **no COMM points**. You still need at least 10 more points including COMM, for example from SOFT (COMM 30), COSMO (METHOD 10, COMM 20) or DDD (METHOD 20, COMM 10).

## Exam format

Facts are as of 2026-10-24, from the [Advanced Level exam page](https://www.isaqb.org/certifications/cpsa-exams/advanced-level-exam/) and the [Examination Rules](https://www.isaqb.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/iSAQB_CPSA_Advanced_Level_Examination_Rules_EN_02-09-2026-1.pdf) dated 2026-09-02. Confirm with your certification body before you apply.

- **Admission:** CPSA-F. At least three years of full-time IT industry experience, including designing and developing at least two different IT systems (exceptions on application, for example for open-source work). At least **70 credit points**, with at least 10 in each of METHOD, TECH and COMM. Evidence: training attendance certificates, a table of your activities and IT systems, a non-disclosure agreement, and a signed declaration not to pass on the task.
- **Credit points:** usually 10 points per training day, at most 30 per course. You must attend at least 75% of a course to earn its points. iSAQB may re-evaluate module point values every 12 months.
- **Exam part 1, written assignment:** a design task for one of three system types: information system, web system or embedded system. You may receive tasks for two system types and choose one within three days. The exam page says **about 40 working hours**; the Examination Rules add that a task usually needs at least 40 hours and can need 60 or more. The documented solution must be returned **within three months**. A late submission fails this part (the rules allow one justified extension of up to three months in exceptional cases, requested in writing before the deadline).
- **Exam part 2, oral defence:** after an electronic plagiarism check, two independent iSAQB examiners review your paper and decide whether you are admitted to the oral examination. They then talk to you (the exam page says by phone), and you explain and defend your design and decisions. The rules say it normally lasts 60 minutes, and the examiners may extend it. The certification body reports the result within two days.
- **Overall time limit:** all exam parts within **twelve months**, or the whole exam fails.
- **Passing:** the examiners confirm that you meet all the requirements, that the solution follows the guidelines, and that it is well documented, explained and defended. There is no percentage score.
- **Fees:** EUR 2,550.00 plus VAT for the exam. EUR 750.00 plus VAT for a repeat (the rules describe this as a one-off chance to revise a failed part within two months). Module training fees are separate and set by the training providers.
- **Languages:** English or German.
- **Validity:** for life.

## What the exam really tests

The Advanced exam has no multiple-choice questions. It tests whether you can **do architecture work and justify it**:

- turning a realistic, partly ambiguous set of requirements into quality goals and quality scenarios;
- making and recording decisions, with alternatives and trade-offs, for a full system;
- producing coherent views (context, building blocks, runtime and deployment) and cross-cutting concepts;
- assessing risks and technical debt, and showing how the design meets the quality goals;
- defending choices under questioning, and admitting and dealing with weaknesses.

The modules feed this through the *competence* you build. For the agentic-AI track, the two transcribed modules work as follows:

- **SWARC4AI** is about *architecting systems that contain AI*. It covers classifying AI and use cases, EU AI Act risk classes and compliance strategies, AI security and safety, data pipelines, ML-specific quality features (robustness, drift, reproducibility, explainability and bias), MLOps, deployment options (SaaS vs. self-hosted vs. embedded), and generative AI architecture: RAG, prompt engineering, agentic workflows, LLM evaluation and cost management.
- **AGENTA** is about *architecting while using AI agents to build software*. It covers AI-assisted decision-making, giving architectural knowledge to coding agents (agent-facing documentation, delivery at the right time), harnesses and controls that keep agents within architectural decisions, extracting architecture from existing code with agents, governance and quality gates for AI usage (legal, traceability, security against prompt injection and tool misuse), and keeping human architectural judgement.

**Common weak points:** solutions that describe technology without linking it to quality goals; missing or shallow decision rationale; inconsistent views; overrunning the three-month window; and in the interview, being unable to explain why an alternative was rejected.

## Study strategy

1. **Pass CPSA-F first.** It is mandatory, and its vocabulary (quality scenarios, views, building blocks and decisions) is the language the examiners expect.
2. **Plan your 70 points across all three areas.** A route that suits this library's goals: **SWARC4AI** (TECH 20 / METHOD 10) + **AGENTA** (TECH 10 / METHOD 20) + one COMM-heavy module such as **SOFT** (COMM 30) or **COSMO**. That gives 90 points with every area covered. **CLOUDINFRA** (TECH 20 / METHOD 10) or **EAM** (METHOD 30) are alternatives that link to the Azure/AWS and TOGAF tracks.
3. **Read each curriculum before booking the training.** The PDFs are public. Map each LG to something you have done, and note the gaps.
4. **Build a practice portfolio.** Document one real system end to end in arc42 style, ideally an AI-enabled one (a RAG service, or an agent with MCP tools). That is the best rehearsal for the 40-hour assignment.
5. **Rehearse the defence.** Ask a colleague to question three of your decisions for ten minutes each.

**How your background helps or misleads**

- *AZ-204, CKAD and the Azure AI track (AI-103, AI-200)* give you concrete material for SWARC4AI chapters 5 and 6: deployment options, monitoring and RAG on managed services. But iSAQB expects vendor-neutral reasoning. Name products only as examples of a decision.
- *Daily use of coding agents* gives you instinct for AGENTA. The module expects you to turn that instinct into **architecture controls**: fitness functions, rule files, review gates and traceability. Using the agent well is not enough.
- *A .NET/Angular background* is a natural source of the "at least two different IT systems" experience the admission requires.

## Differences from related exams

- **CPSA-A vs CPSA-F.** CPSA-F is a knowledge exam: about 40 questions in 75 minutes, 60% to pass. CPSA-A is an assessment of real work: a task of about 40 hours plus an oral defence of about an hour, after 70 credit points of training. Exam fees also differ a lot (CPSA-A is EUR 2,550 + VAT).
- **CPSA-A vs OGEA-102 (TOGAF Practitioner).** Both are practitioner-level, but OGEA-102 is a 90-minute, 8-scenario open-book exam on the TOGAF method at enterprise level. CPSA-A has you *design* one system and defend it. Only CPSA-A asks you to produce an architecture. The **EAM** module bridges the two.
- **CPSA-A (SWARC4AI and AGENTA) vs AIGP.** SWARC4AI chapter 2 overlaps with AIGP Domains II–IV (EU AI Act classification, data protection, AI governance principles), but from the architect's side: how compliance shapes the design. AIGP covers the governance professional's side: policy, impact assessments and oversight.
- **CPSA-A vs Claude Certified Architect – Professional and NCP-AAI.** The vendor exams test agent architecture on one stack (Claude, or NVIDIA NIM and NeMo) through multiple-choice items. AGENTA and SWARC4AI teach the vendor-neutral principles, and CPSA-A proves them in a written design.
- **CPSA-A vs AZ-305 and SAP-C02/SAP-C03.** The cloud professional exams test service-selection judgement under time pressure. CPSA-A tests the reasoning and documentation behind a design, with no time pressure but with an examiner questioning you.

## Exam-day notes

There is no single exam day, so plan the whole timeline instead:

- **When you receive the task:** you have three days to choose between system types if you received two. Choose the domain where you can write quality scenarios most convincingly, not the one that seems most fashionable.
- **Plan the 40 to 60 hours:** a workable split is about 15% understanding the task and clarifying assumptions, 50% design and decisions, 25% documentation and consistency checks, and 10% review. Write down every assumption. Examiners reward assumptions that are explicit and justified.
- **Submit early rather than late.** Missing the three-month deadline fails that part outright.
- **For the oral examination:** plan for about 60 minutes. Reread your own paper the day before. Prepare a two-minute summary of the architecture and its top three risks. When challenged, explain the trade-off rather than defending the choice at any cost.
- **Confidentiality:** you sign an NDA and a declaration not to pass on the task. Do not discuss it in forums or with AI tools that keep data.
